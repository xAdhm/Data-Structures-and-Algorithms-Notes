# Trees and Graphs - Nodes, Vertices, and Edges

Trees and graphs are probably the most common category of interview question — unlike hash maps or "array"/"string" (too broad to be a distinct "type"), trees and graphs are genuinely their own topic, and a huge share of interview problems are built entirely around them.

## Revisiting the node concept
A [[Linked Lists - Overview|node]] (as introduced for linked lists) is an abstract data type with two parts:
1. **Data** — anything: an integer, boolean, hash map, custom object, etc.
2. **Pointers to other nodes**

## What is a graph?
A **graph** is any collection of nodes and their pointers to other nodes. Linked lists and trees are both, technically, *types of graphs*. Graphs as a subject are extremely broad — there's an entire academic field, **graph theory**, dedicated to them.

Even though a tree is technically a type of graph, trees and graphs are treated as **separate topics** in algorithm interviews — graphs are the more advanced/difficult topic, so this course covers trees first.

## Terminology
- Nodes of a graph are also called **vertices**
- The pointers connecting them are called **edges**
- Graphically: vertices are usually circles, edges are lines/arrows connecting them (same visual convention used for linked lists)
